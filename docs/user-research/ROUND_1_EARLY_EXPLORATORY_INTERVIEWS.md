# Round 1: Early Exploratory Interviews

## Purpose and evidence status

Three students took part in short verbal interviews during the initial project phase. The interviews explored whether the proposed restaurant-capacity concept appeared useful and what information a potential diner would need before acting.

No recording, transcript, individual demographic form, or contemporaneous answer sheet was retained. The detailed notes below are therefore **retrospective paraphrases** produced from the facilitator's recollection, the confirmed interview format, and the product themes carried into subsequent design work. They are not verbatim quotations and must not be used as statistical evidence.

## Participants

| ID | Relationship to project | Broad status | Perspective | Other demographics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | External | Student | Potential diner | Not recorded |
| E02 | External | Student | Potential diner | Not recorded |
| E03 | External | Student | Potential diner | Not recorded |

E01--E03 were three distinct people and did not participate in Round 3. Age, gender, nationality, accessibility needs, prior restaurant-app usage, and restaurant-industry experience were not recorded. No restaurant operator was interviewed.

## Method

The facilitator presented the early concept and asked three confirmed core questions:

1. What do you think of this concept?
2. Do you think people would use it? Why or why not?
3. Would you personally use it?

The interview was informal and conversational. The later prompts about decision information and hesitation shown below reconstruct the follow-up themes; their exact wording cannot be verified.

## Retrospective interview records

### E01 — immediate availability

| Topic | Retrospective answer summary |
|---|---|
| First reaction | The idea seemed useful when someone wanted to eat soon but did not know which nearby restaurant could seat them. |
| Likely use | Short-notice diners could avoid checking several restaurants or arriving to find no table. |
| Personal use | The participant could imagine using it for an unplanned meal after class or group work. |
| Decision information | Restaurant type, distance, current free-table count, and a simple quiet/busy indication. |
| Main hesitation | Availability could become outdated before the diner arrived. |

Design theme: immediate availability should be easy to scan and current enough to support a quick decision.

### E02 — budget and offer relevance

| Topic | Retrospective answer summary |
|---|---|
| First reaction | Live availability was useful, and a discount could make a nearby restaurant more attractive when no venue had been chosen. |
| Likely use | An offer would be more useful when it matched location, food preference, and budget rather than being a generic promotion. |
| Personal use | The participant could imagine using a time-limited offer if there was still enough time to decide and travel. |
| Decision information | Discount, cuisine, price level, distance, expiry time, and what accepting the offer would do. |
| Main hesitation | Irrelevant notifications or uncertainty about whether accepting immediately created a booking. |

Design theme: Flash Deals should be targeted and limited, with clear consequences and expiry.

### E03 — reachability

| Topic | Retrospective answer summary |
|---|---|
| First reaction | Knowing a table existed was helpful only if the diner could reach the restaurant before it was released. |
| Likely use | The concept was more credible when availability was connected to realistic travel time. |
| Personal use | The participant could imagine using it when walking or public transport made straight-line distance misleading. |
| Decision information | Distance, travel mode, estimated arrival time, hold window, and whether arrival appeared feasible. |
| Main hesitation | Booking and then discovering that the journey took longer than expected. |

Design theme: reachability should be checked before a diner commits to an immediate booking.

## Cross-interview synthesis

The three reconstructed themes formed a connected student-diner problem:

- uncertainty about whether a table is available now;
- uncertainty about whether the diner can reach it in time; and
- a need for relevant, affordable offers without notification noise.

The interviews were exploratory. They supported continuing with the concept and helped the team organise early needs, but they did not validate market demand, willingness to pay, Flash Deal economics, or restaurant operations.

## Persona and requirements derived from Round 1

The team synthesised the early themes into a design persona. This persona is a fictional composite, not one participant and not an additional research record.

### Composite persona: Sam Lee

| Field | Design synthesis |
|---|---|
| Role | Postgraduate student with an irregular class and group-work schedule |
| Context | Often chooses where to eat after class, during project work, or while travelling with friends; usually walks or uses public transport and has a limited dining budget |
| Goals | Find a suitable nearby restaurant with a table available soon, know whether arrival is feasible, and occasionally obtain a relevant discount |
| Frustrations | Listings without live availability, distance that does not reflect travel time, noisy generic promotions, and unclear booking language |
| Needs | Current table availability, cuisine and price context, distance, ETA, hold window, targeted Flash Deals, explicit expiry, and clear booking consequences |
| Technology comfort | High; familiar with maps, restaurant search, mobile booking, and notifications, but expects fast and clear feedback |
| Persona statement | Design statement, not a quotation: “Show me a table I can afford and actually reach before it is gone.” |

### Traceability into product design

| Synthesised need | Product direction |
|---|---|
| Immediate certainty | Real-time table availability and clear quiet/busy state |
| Reachable choice | A focused 1.5 km discovery radius plus ETA and hold-window validation |
| Budget relevance | Price preferences and limited discounts |
| Low-noise promotion | Private, preference-aware Flash Deals rather than public mass promotion |
| Clear commitment | Explicit Book/Claim consequences, expiry, confirmation, and next action |

These directions were expressed in later user stories and acceptance criteria. They were refined again after technical feasibility feedback and the decision to separate the diner-facing mobile experience from the merchant-facing web dashboard.

## Limitations

- Detailed answers were reconstructed retrospectively and are not verbatim evidence.
- The sample was small, informal, and entirely student-based.
- Demographic data were not collected beyond student status.
- No restaurant operator participated.
- The round explored perceived usefulness only; it did not measure behaviour, usability, demand, pricing, or commercial impact.
