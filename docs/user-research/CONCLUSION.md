# Conclusion

## Overall synthesis

The three rounds served different purposes. Round 1 explored whether the problem was meaningful to potential student diners and informed the first persona, user stories, and acceptance criteria. Round 2 exposed internal product and integration risks before demonstration. Round 3 tested the end-to-end diner and merchant workflows with four evaluators and produced a prioritised usability action list.

Across the two participant-based rounds there were **seven distinct people**: three early exploratory interviewees and four later usability evaluators. The five-person internal team review is reported separately and is not counted as external-user evidence.

The strongest supported outcome is that the prototype's two-sided loop was operable in the test setting: users could discover restaurants, inspect availability and ETA, book or cancel, claim a Flash Deal, create a merchant campaign, and inspect the resulting state. The research also identified important weaknesses in first-use positioning, mobile readability, commitment language, navigation continuity, confirmation feedback, and campaign-state consistency.

## Product impact

The research contributed to the following design directions:

- live table availability combined with ETA and a hold window;
- a focused nearby discovery experience;
- preference-aware private Flash Deals;
- separate diner-facing mobile and merchant-facing web experiences;
- clearer visual and interaction consistency across both sides; and
- targeted fixes for rendering and campaign-state synchronisation.

Booking confirmation was reported implemented before the final demonstration, while first-use explanation, broader completion feedback, and restaurant-operator validation remained incomplete or were not independently re-tested.

## Evidence limitations

The findings are formative and should not be generalised to the target market. All external participants were students, no restaurant operator participated, detailed demographics were not consistently collected, and the early interviews had no retained transcript. The later test used a desktop responsive viewport rather than native mobile applications and did not measure task time, accessibility compliance, market demand, willingness to pay, real ETA accuracy, or revenue impact.

## Next evaluation steps

Future work should recruit independent diners and restaurant owners or managers, collect a consistent minimum demographic profile, test on native devices, measure task success and completion time, validate route and availability accuracy, and conduct a documented regression round after fixes.

A separate 10-question usability questionnaire was also released to collect perceptions of system use; its anonymised statistical results will be presented in a dedicated results file rather than in this interview evidence package.
